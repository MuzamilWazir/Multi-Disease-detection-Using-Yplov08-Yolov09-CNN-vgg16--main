The problem

Manual inspection doesn't scale.

Apricots are a valuable cash crop, but disease identification is still done by eye, fruit by fruit. Across large harvests this is slow, inconsistent, and often catches disease only after it has already spread. This project builds an automated alternative — a model that can screen fruit as fast as a camera can see it.

What it detects

Three classes, trained on real orchard photos.

Every image in the dataset was collected directly from the field and manually annotated by hand — marking the exact location and class of each fruit before training.

HEALTHY · 0.89

Healthy

No visible disease markers — smooth skin, even color, no lesions or fungal growth.

BROWN_ROT · 0.92

Brown Rot

A fungal infection producing concentric brown lesions, often spreading rapidly across the fruit surface.

SHOT_HOLE · 0.91

Shot Hole

Small circular lesions that can dry out and drop away, leaving a distinctive perforated pattern.

How it works

From orchard to model, in four steps.

01

Field data collection

Apricots were photographed directly from the field across varying lighting, angles, and ripeness stages.

02

Manual annotation

Each image was hand-labeled with bounding boxes marking disease class and location — the ground truth the model learns from.

03

Model training

A YOLOv8 object detection model was trained on the annotated dataset to localize and classify each condition.

04

Comparative evaluation

Results were benchmarked against alternative detection approaches, with YOLOv8 achieving the strongest results.
